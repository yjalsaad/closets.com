# VIP room film: "THE CLOSETS — THE JOURNEY OF AMBITION" (الخزائن — حكاية طموح)

A 124 second documentary-style film for the big screen of the VIP room. Files are in `public/yas-bloom/film/` (served as `/yas-bloom/film/`); the VIP room code reads `film.json` and is wired by the showroom branch, not here.

## Read this first

- **Every picture in the film is AI-generated illustration. None of it is real footage of the company, its people, its factories, its showrooms or its projects.** The people are fictional. The end card says so on screen ("Illustrative imagery generated with AI" in English and Arabic), and `film.json` repeats it in `notes`.
- **What the film actually is:** 3 true AI video shots (5 s each, about 14 s on screen) and 40 moving stills (33 generated photographs, some used twice with a different framing) animated locally with slow camera moves, dissolves, a match cut, warm light-leak flashes and floating dust. It is a hybrid, not eight scenes of video: 50 credits do not buy that (see the upgrade list).
- **The narration is a synthetic voice** reading the owner's Arabic script word for word. Nobody was cloned; a library voice was used. The music is generated, not licensed.
- **Facts stated** come from the owner's brief for this film and nothing was added: a small woodworking workshop in Bahrain; two factories; more than five branches; Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UAE; more than 10,000 projects; European machinery; quality and safety standards; a digital transformation that connects the listed functions. No certificate, standard name, number, logo or date is shown or invented.
- **Open point for management:** `docs/showroom3d/VIP-ROOM-OWNER-INPUTS.md` records that the website itself gives conflicting figures (factories or one workshop, 4 showrooms, "10K+" and "500+" homes) and the VIP room shows those four facts as "to be confirmed". This film states the owner's figures outright. Confirm them in `src/content/facts.js` so the room and the film agree.

## Frames reviewed and known weaknesses

The finished 1080p file was checked with ffprobe (H.264 High, 1920x1080, 24 fps, 124.0 s, AAC stereo 48 kHz) and 35 frames across all eight scenes plus the title, the 10,000 reveal, the system graph and both end-card states were extracted and looked at. Sound was checked by measurement only (it cannot be listened to here): -16.0 LUFS integrated, peak -1.7 dBFS, no clipping; the narration was transcribed and matches the script.

What holds up: the workshop scenes (faces, hands on the pencil and saw, thobe and wrapped ghutra), the three video shots (stable anatomy, no morphing), the interiors, the aerials, and all local typography (spelling exact in both languages, Arabic shaped and ordered correctly).

Weakest points, worst first:

1. `6a` boardroom (60.6-66.3 s and 75.2 s): the wall screen and the papers carry pseudo-writing, and in the tight crop on the founder a standing man is cut off at the chest.
2. `6c` safety briefing (73.9 s): seven near-identical workers in a row with the same pose; reads as generated.
3. `4b` Saudi showroom (44.3 s): the shemagh of the man on the left drapes too heavily; `4d` and `4c`: the consultant and the engineer look European rather than Gulf.
4. `3c` / `7c`: the line worker and warehouse worker are generic, not recognisably Bahraini (the brief asks for Bahraini focus; the factory floor is mixed here).
5. `8b` team portrait (107.9 s): slightly posed, and one ghutra sits unusually high.
6. Scenes 4 to 7 are stills: people do not move, only the camera does.
7. Country labels are small for a large screen (30 px Latin, 37 px Arabic at 1080p).
8. For about one second near 39 s the music's own pause coincides with a gap in the narration, so the track is nearly silent over the CNC shot; the final chord under the end card is quiet.

None of these was regenerated: 2.06 credits remain, the fixes are prompt retries at 0.5 each with no guarantee, and the stills were already reviewed one by one before the edit (one, the first Qatar frame, was rejected and replaced then).

## Limitations

- Most of the film is stills with camera moves. On a large screen the difference from live footage is visible, most of all in scenes 4 to 7 where people stand still.
- The founder appears young (scenes 1 and 2) and older (scenes 6 and 8) from one reference image; the likeness is close, not exact. Other people are not recurring characters.
- Traditional dress was prompted per country and checked by eye (one Qatar frame was rejected and redone). It has not been reviewed by a Gulf national; the Saudi and Qatari frames deserve that look before the film is shown to guests.
- Skylines behind the showrooms are AI impressions of Manama, Riyadh, Doha and Dubai, not accurate views. The factories, showrooms and homes are invented.
- Screens and papers inside the pictures carry meaningless marks instead of text; at 1080p some are visible as pseudo-writing (boardroom screen, checklist).
- The voice is a synthetic, multilingual library voice. Its Arabic was verified by transcription only; nobody has listened to it. A native listener must judge accent, the Gulf-dialect words and the tone before release. The music was likewise judged only by its loudness curve.
- Machine shots show an operator standing close to an open CNC table, which a safety officer may dislike.
- 1080p at 1 Mbit/s is tight; fast dissolves soften briefly.

The edit lives in `build_film.py` (`SHOTS`, `NARR_SEGMENTS`, the overlay functions); `--frames 3.5,53.4` renders single frames for checking, `--edl` prints the edit list. To replace a still with a video clip, put the clip in `clips/`, change that row to `kind="video"` and rebuild. The source PNGs and clips are not committed (about 230 MB); the CDN URLs in the ledger are the backup, so download and archive them soon in case they expire.
